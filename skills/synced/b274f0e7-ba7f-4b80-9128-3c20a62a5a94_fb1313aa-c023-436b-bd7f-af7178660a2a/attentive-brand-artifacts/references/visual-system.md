## Logo

Primary mark: the Attentive wordmark.

Secondary mark: the symbol. Use it for favicons, avatars, watermarks, graphic devices, or when Attentive has already been established. Do not pair the symbol and wordmark into a new lockup.

Logo colors:

- Poppyseed `#1E1C1C`
- Yuzu `#FFD60B`
- Meringue `#FCFAEE`

Rules:

- Use only core neutrals and primary yellow for logo applications.
- Do not use secondary or tertiary colors on the logo.
- On light backgrounds, use Poppyseed.
- On dark backgrounds, use Meringue.
- Use Yuzu only where contrast and brand context are clear.
- Wordmark clear space: use the character width from the wordmark as the unit.
- Partnership lockups: clear space around full lockup equals `0.5x`; distance between logos equals `2x`, where `x` is based on the wordmark's `a`.
- Social avatar: keep about 15% margin between mark and container edge.
- Channel lockups can clarify platform breadth, but must not replace the primary logo where brand recognition is already established.

Do not:

- Recreate, outline, distort, rotate, add effects to, or recolor the logo.
- Put the symbol in a lockup with the wordmark.
- Use low-contrast logo placements.
- Layer the logo over noisy imagery without a clear contrast decision.

Graphic logo system:

- The circle inside the wordmark can become a framing device for photography.
- The full wordmark can be used as a framing/masking element for human imagery in high-brand moments.
- Use these treatments sparingly; they are brand moments, not default decoration.

Color hierarchy:

- Core communications: Poppyseed, Yuzu, Meringue, Latte.
- Backgrounds and text: Poppyseed/Meringue first.
- Secondary brand content, web, social, and product: use supporting colors selectively.
- Colorful text is not recommended. Default to Poppyseed on Meringue.
- Never rely on color alone to communicate meaning.
- Re-test contrast if opacity, overlay, blur, or gradients are introduced.

Gradients:

- Use gradients as subtle support, not as the brand itself.
- Build most gradients from Yuzu and warm neutrals.
- Use as soft highlights behind key elements or branded hero backdrops.
- Product/message overlays can use a soft highlight plus a 50% blur/drop shadow treatment.

Do not:

- Layer arbitrary color on color.
- Create pairings outside the primary palette without a reason.
- Make multicolor dashboards.
- Use colorful body text.
- Use generic SaaS blue/purple gradients.

## Typography

Brand fonts:

- Primary serif: Academica Book by Storm Type Foundry.
- Primary serif emphasis: Academica Italic.
- Secondary sans: ABC Diatype Regular and Bold.

Google/system fallbacks:

- Serif fallback: Libre Baskerville.
- Sans fallback: Inter.

Use:

- Headlines: Academica Book. Add Academica Italic for emphasis.
- Headline italic balance: roughly 15-20% of the headline, not multiple phrases.
- Body, UI, captions, labels, supporting information: ABC Diatype or Inter.
- Bold sans only for emphasis, callouts, nav labels, or hierarchy.
- Single-sentence headlines should be sentence case and have no periods.
- Oversized headline style is reserved for brand-defining statements and key moments.

Known type specs from guide:

- Hero headline: Academica regular and italic, 90% line spacing, `-3%` tracking.
- Body: ABC Diatype regular/bold, 120% line spacing, `0.5%` tracking.
- Large display example: 110% line height and `-3%` letter spacing.
- Body example: 130% line height and `-1%` letter spacing.
- Avoid too tight: 100% line height, `-2%` tracking.
- Avoid too loose: 200% line height, `3%` tracking.

Type scale from guide:

- HXL: 74pt, 90% spacing
- HL: 56pt, 90% spacing
- HM: 42pt, 90% spacing
- HS: 31pt, 110% spacing
- HXS: 24pt, 120% spacing
- P1: 17pt, 120% spacing
- P2: 13pt, 130% spacing

Digital approximation:

- HXL: 96px / 0.9
- HL: 72px / 0.95
- HM: 56px / 1.0
- HS: 40px / 1.1
- HXS: 28px / 1.2
- P1: 18px / 1.4
- P2: 14px / 1.45

Type cleanup:

- Avoid widows in headlines.
- Keep ragged text intentional.
- Use type as image only in high-brand moments.
- Word-list treatments can use action verbs such as Measure, Integrate, Personalize, Deliver, Drive.

## Buttons, Tags, And UI

- Primary CTA: Yuzu fill with Poppyseed text.
- Secondary CTA: quiet outline or text link.
- Tags/metadata: subtle, structured containers.
- Metadata container system: 10pt internal spacing, 7.7px radius, 100% corner smoothing.
- Product snippets: 14pt corner radius, 24pt margins, natural pattern backgrounds.

## Photography

Art direction:

- Natural, candid, genuinely human.
- Brief real moments, not staged scenes.
- Everyday settings across different walks of life.
- Mid to close crops that focus on expression, gesture, and lived experience.
- Natural light whenever possible; direct flash only when it fits.
- Devices should feel integrated into the environment, not hero objects.
- Screens and UI should not dominate the photo.

Marketer imagery:

- Show real working environments.
- Capture desks, meetings, laptops, phones, dashboards, campaigns, and messages in motion.
- Show focus, momentum, problem-solving, and small moments of clarity.
- Do not stage stock office culture.

Texture imagery:

- Use natural imagery as atmospheric support for complex/product-focused moments.
- Use soft, dreamy lighting, natural gradients, organic shadows, subtle pattern.
- Create pattern backdrops by starting with out-of-focus nature imagery, applying motion blur, and adding light noise.

Do not:

- Use overly staged photography.
- Use cool, moody, corporate stock images.
- Use forced-fun group scenes.

## Product Visuals

Two modes:

- Product snippets: cropped, true-to-UI moments showing specific features or states.
- Abstracted communication moments: SMS, email, push, or reply moments between marketers and consumers.

Use product snippets when clarity and credibility matter: product marketing, sales decks, feature callouts, case studies.

Phone shells:

- Use iOS-style phone shells for functional/product-driven contexts.
- Good for product marketing, feature explanations, sales materials, case studies, and decks.

Floating messages:

- In cinematic or emotion-led moments, segment messages and place them directly in the composition.
- Messages can float, stack, or interact with photography.
- Message tails should be part of the design.
- Add light progressive blur to segmented threads when blending into scenes.

Email and product screens:

- Simplify and refine real UI.
- Preserve hierarchy and core components.
- Remove decorative or extraneous interface elements.
- Use reduced screens to isolate key modules, flows, or moments.
- Use progressive blur to soften the boundary between product layers and photography.

Context halo:

- Put a person or moment at center.
- Surround it with layered signals such as location, send time, segments, and live messaging.
- Use this to show personalization as a coordinated system, not a single automation.

Payoff:

- Success stories should focus on the human response: reply bubble, conversion, order confirmed, ticket purchased.
- Do not make the dashboard the hero when the message worked is the real point.

## Data Visualization

Principles:

- Start with the outcome. Lead with the metric that matters most.
- One core takeaway per frame.
- Clarity over density.
- Use brand shapes intentionally: circular, radial, segmented, rounded.
- Charts should feel designed, not default.
- Use generous spacing.
- Neutrals and Yuzu anchor the composition.
- Use secondary colors only for comparison or contrast.
- Color signals hierarchy, not decoration.
- Simplify until the chart is immediate and intuitive.
- Show progress and momentum with arrows, incremental fills, expanding circles, or progressive gradients.

Good chart forms:

- Large metric callout with small support copy.
- Donut/radial progress.
- Segmented gauge.
- Small line chart in a soft card.
- Minimal bar chart with Yuzu gradient fills.
- Bubble comparison with a small number of categories.
- Context halo around customer/marketer signals.

Avoid:

- Dense dashboards.
- Default spreadsheet styling.
- Multicolor palettes with no hierarchy.
- Charts that need a paragraph to explain.

## Illustration And Icons

Illustration is a secondary expression. It supports photography and product storytelling; it should not replace photography in high-visibility hero placements.

Principles:

- Supports, never competes with primary imagery.
- Adds warmth without noise.
- Uses repetition, geometry, and disciplined color.
- Modular forms repeat to create rhythm.
- Use two brand colors paired with black or white.
- Avoid gradients, blending, and excessive accents.
- Rounded corners should echo the logo symbol.
- Keep grid, line weight, radius, and geometry consistent.
- Stroke: 2pt, Poppyseed or Meringue.
- Use neutral plus one or two colors.

Utility icons:

- Export at 24x24.
- Use SVG.
- Join layers in a union named `Primary fill`.
- Do not bake size variants into the icon source.

## Motion

Motion templates include title cards, end cards, lower thirds, logo animation, type animation, and product animations.

Motion should extend the illustration system: structured, warm, restrained, and supportive. Do not turn motion into generic AI shimmer.
