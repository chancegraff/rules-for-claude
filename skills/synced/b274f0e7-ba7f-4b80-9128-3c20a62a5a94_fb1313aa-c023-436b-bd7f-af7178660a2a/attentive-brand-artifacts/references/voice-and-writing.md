## Brand Foundation

Brand idea: Marketing made personal.

Platform statement: We identify, personalize, and act across every channel so teams can turn messages into moments that drive revenue and loyalty.

Product statement: SMS, email, RCS, and push notifications—each powered by mobile-first identity and agentic AI—give marketers the tools to reach the right person, at the right moment, every time.

Brand belief: At its best, marketing feels like a helpful conversation with someone who truly pays attention. Attentive understands that personalized marketing requires both powerful technology and the right people behind it.

Vision: Redefine the way marketing connects brands and people.

Mission: Create bespoke shopping experiences between people and the brands they love.

Customer value proposition: Attentive enables marketers to deliver relevant, personalized customer experiences that drive revenue and foster lasting brand loyalty.

Brand positioning: Attentive empowers customers to turn messages into moments that drive action, revenue, and loyalty—with support that helps them succeed.

Brand attributes: Perceptive • Deliberate • Agile • Human

## Narrative Lens

The world doesn't shop in straight lines. People move between tabs and texts, scrolls and screens, impulses and intentions. Attentive helps brands move beyond mechanics and create experiences that feel genuinely personal.

Attentive marketing listens before it speaks. It arrives with intention, not interruption. It feels like a relationship and performs better because of it.

Marketing made personal does not mean using someone's name. It means understanding who they are, what they need, and when a message will actually help.

Useful language:
- Marketing made personal.
- The right message at the right moment.
- Messages that arrive, not interrupt.
- Every message earns its moment.
- Marketing that listens before it speaks.
- Relevance builds trust, and trust drives growth.
- All the data, fewer second thoughts.

Avoid reducing the brand to:
- Automation for automation's sake.
- Generic omnichannel orchestration language.
- Hype about AI with no human judgment.
- Claims that sound like surveillance or over-personalization.

## Personality and Voice

Voice does not change. Tone adapts by audience, context, and content type.

### Perceptive

Meaning: We see what others miss. We read signals, spot patterns, and understand what drives people—not just what they do, but why.

Sounds: Informed, context-aware, situational, attuned, nuanced, timely.

How it shows up:
- Lead with insight before product.
- Ground messages in what matters now.
- Choose specificity over generality.
- Show understanding through restraint, not volume.

Write this: "Your customers are already telling you what they need. Attentive helps you recognize the signal and respond while the moment still matters."
Not this: "Our platform has advanced segmentation features."

### Deliberate

Meaning: Every word has a job. We act with direction in product, messaging, and moments.

Sounds: Intentional, assured, decisive, measured, direct.

How it shows up:
- Connect every claim to an outcome.
- Cut anything that doesn't earn its place.
- Use clear, confident language without overstatement.
- Make decisions visible through precise wording.

Write this: "Every message should have a reason to exist—and a clear outcome to drive."
Not this: "Unlock powerful solutions that maximize engagement."

### Agile

Meaning: We move with people and adapt in real time across channels, audiences, and moments.

Sounds: Adaptive, continuous, steady, responsive, flexible with control.

How it shows up:
- Write for motion, not static journeys.
- Connect moments across channels.
- Show how experiences evolve without friction.
- Emphasize continuity and relevance.

Write this: "Every interaction informs the next, so each channel gets smarter instead of starting over."
Not this: "Automate your marketing workflows across multiple channels."

### Human

Meaning: Technology should amplify human understanding, not replace it. Behind every data point is a person.

Sounds: Conversational, curious, approachable, warm, thoughtful, real.

How it shows up:
- Write like you're advising a colleague.
- Avoid jargon, hype, and scripts.
- Balance warmth with point of view.
- Respect people's time, attention, and boundaries.

Write this: "Your customers want to feel known—not followed around the internet."
Not this: "Leverage data-driven orchestration to optimize consumer touchpoints."

## Tone by Situation

### Blog posts and thought leadership
Tone: Curious, strategic, forward-looking.
Use when explaining market shifts, sharing perspective, or helping marketers understand what's changing.
Example: "Here's what the shift to omnichannel is actually doing to customer expectations—and what smart brands are doing about it."

### Product marketing
Tone: Direct, specific, outcome-focused.
Use when explaining product value, connecting capabilities to business results, writing web copy, one-pagers, launch copy, and sales enablement.
Example: "Know who's shopping. Reach them everywhere. Drive results that actually move the number."

### Customer support and documentation
Tone: Patient, practical, clear.
Use when giving instructions, explaining steps, or reducing friction.
Example: "Let's take this one step at a time. Here's exactly what to do first."

### Sales materials
Tone: Confident, consultative, informed.
Use when advising prospects, framing opportunities, or explaining strategy.
Example: "Here's what we're seeing work for brands like yours, and how we'd think about your specific situation."

### Brand campaigns and awareness
Tone: Warm, human, emotionally resonant.
Use when building belief, creating campaign lines, or introducing the brand promise.
Example: "Marketing that feels like a helpful conversation with someone who truly pays attention."

## Brand Copy vs. Platform Copy

### Brand copy
Brand copy leads with the emotional promise. It is warm, human, and outcome-oriented in a customer-feeling sense.

Use brand copy for: campaigns, brand moments, awareness content, narrative storytelling, manifestos, social thought starters.

Example: "Marketing made personal."
Example: "Turn messages into moments people remember."

### Platform and product copy
Platform and product copy leads with results and proof. It is direct, specific, and outcome-oriented in a business-performance sense.

Use platform and product copy for: web pages, sales decks, category pages, feature pages, product announcements, competitive positioning.

Example: "Attentive identifies shoppers, personalizes every touchpoint, and acts across SMS, email, RCS, and push."
Example: "2.4x higher conversion when every channel works together."

## Platform Pillars

### Omnichannel
Promise: Deliver seamless omnichannel experiences at scale.

Core idea: Omnichannel is SMS, email, RCS, and push working together as one system—where what happens in one channel informs and improves the next.

How to talk about it:
- Start with what the system does before using the word "omnichannel."
- Describe harmony, coordination, and continuity.
- Show how channels inform each other in real time.
- Focus on less noise for customers and better performance for brands.
- In brand-facing assets, describe the behavior first ("SMS, email, RCS, and push working in harmony"), then introduce the word.
- "Omnichannel Marketing Platform" is the category label. Use it when staking a position in the competitive landscape.

Write this: "Attentive brings SMS, email, RCS, and push into harmony—one system where every message informs the next."
Not this: "Add email to SMS with our omnichannel solution."

Avoid:
- Framing omnichannel as simply adding more channels.
- Saying "bolt-on" or "add-on."
- Using "omnichannel" without explaining what it means for the audience.
- Connecting agentic AI to omnichannel is correct—they are two layers of one system.

Brand-to-platform bridge: Agile → Emphasize real-time response, cross-channel coordination, continuous optimization.

### Agentic AI
Promise: Maximize revenue with always-on AI.

Core idea: Agentic AI acts on the marketer's behalf. It continuously plans, executes, and optimizes across channels in real time.

Term usage:
- Use "agentic AI" for the system, capability, platform-level positioning, or product/feature technology.
- Use "AI agents" when describing the individual workers inside the system, what is happening behind the scenes, or more relatable, action-oriented storytelling.
- Both are correct and interchangeable, but the emphasis differs.

Write this: "Attentive's agentic AI continuously plans, executes, and optimizes messaging on your behalf."
Write this: "AI agents work in the background to grow your list, understand your shoppers, and drive conversions."

Do not:
- Position AI as a replacement for marketers—it is a force multiplier.
- Overhype AI as magic.
- Use vague claims like "revolutionary AI will transform everything."

Brand-to-platform bridge: Deliberate → Focus on outcomes and actions—what changes, what improves, what it drives.

### Identity
Promise: Identify and learn from high-intent shoppers across every channel.

Core idea: Personalization starts with recognition. Identity is how Attentive recognizes who's visiting a brand's website and connects that visit back to a real customer profile—their email, phone number, browsing behavior, purchase history, and more.

How to talk about it:
- Lead with recognition, not technical mechanics.
- Explain what Identity makes possible for the marketer.
- Connect Identity to omnichannel and agentic AI—they depend on each other.
- Keep the explanation simple, logical, and valuable.

Write this: "Identity is how Attentive recognizes who's shopping and turns that recognition into relevant, well-timed messages."
Not this: "Our server-side architecture improves probabilistic matching."

Do not:
- Use Identity interchangeably with identification rates—they are different.
- Go deep into cookie types, server-side architecture, or probabilistic matching in brand-facing content.
- Frame it negatively: "without Identity, nothing works."
- Frame it positively: "Identity is what makes it all work."

Capitalize Identity when referring to Attentive's product or capability. Lowercase identity when referring to the general concept.

Brand-to-platform bridge: Perceptive → Lead with recognition and identification—we see what others miss.

## Writing Principles

Educate: Give readers what they need to know, not just what Attentive wants to say. Share useful insight they may not find on their own.

Guide: Lead readers through ideas, processes, products, and next steps in a friendly, helpful way.

Empower: Help readers understand what is possible with Attentive and feel confident taking action.

Be clear: Use straightforward words and sentences. Avoid jargon and complicated phrasing.

Be useful: Know the audience, the purpose, and what the reader needs to walk away understanding.

Be adaptable: Adjust tone for the situation while keeping the voice consistent.

## Core Writing Rules

### Write like a human
If you wouldn't say it in a conversation, don't write it.

Use: "We'll help you understand what your customers want and respond in the moments that matter."
Avoid: "Our platform enables cross-channel orchestration capabilities that optimize engagement touchpoints."

### Embrace brevity
Every word should earn its place.

Before: "In order to help brands create more meaningful customer experiences, Attentive provides a platform that enables marketers to use customer data across many different channels."
After: "Attentive helps brands use customer data to create more meaningful experiences across every channel."

### Make the customer the hero
Lead with the reader's goals, customers, and outcomes—not Attentive's features.

Write: "Your customers want relevant messages that feel helpful, not intrusive."
Not: "Our platform has advanced personalization features."

### Be positive and supportive
Focus on what customers can achieve, not what they lack or risk losing.

Write: "Here's how to build stronger customer relationships across every channel."
Not: "If you don't adopt omnichannel now, you'll fall behind."

### Use contractions
Contractions help Attentive sound natural and human.

Use: we're, you're, it's, don't, can't, they're.
Avoid uncommon contractions in formal or external content: that'd, they'd when it feels awkward.

### Use active voice
Active: "Attentive helps marketers recognize more shoppers."
Passive: "More shoppers are recognized by Attentive."

## Grammar and Mechanics

### Capitalization
- Title case: case study titles, press release titles, blog post titles, webinar titles, one-pager titles. Rule: capitalize nouns, pronouns, adjectives, verbs, and all words four or more letters long. Capitalize both words in hyphenated terms.
- Sentence case: website headlines, website subheads, blog subheads, ad headlines, email subject lines, CTA buttons. Rule: capitalize only the first word and proper nouns.
- When in doubt, use sentence case. Never use all caps.

### Commas
- Use the Oxford comma in lists of three or more.
- Do not use a comma before "and" for a list of two.
- Example: "SMS, RCS, and email" — not "SMS, RCS and email."

### Periods
- Use periods in full sentences in body copy.
- Do not use periods in headlines, subheads, or CTA buttons.

### Em dashes
- Use a true em dash with no spaces: "Every signal matters—especially when timing is everything."
- Use for a brief aside, a thought extension, or added emphasis.
- Do not overuse. If a sentence gets too long, split it.

### Ampersands
- Use sparingly: in official company names, social posts with tight character count, or decks where space is limited.
- Avoid in long-form content, email body copy, or website body copy when "and" fits.

### Hyphens
Use a hyphen when two or more words act as one idea before a noun.

Common pairs:
- real-time (adjective) / real time (noun)
- on-demand (adjective) / on demand (noun)
- sign-up (noun or adjective) / sign up (verb)
- opt-in (noun) / opt in (verb)
- opt-out (noun) / opt out (verb)
- set-up (noun) / set up (verb)
- third-party (adjective) / third party (noun)
- best-in-class (adjective) / best in class (noun)

### Numbers
- Spell out numbers less than 10 in body copy.
- Use numerals for 10 and above.
- Spell out a number when it begins a sentence (unless it begins a title or headline).
- Use commas for numbers over three digits: 1,000 / 24,500.
- Use the % symbol, not "percent."
- In tight spaces: 1K / 150K / 1M / 1B.

### Dates and time
- Spell out days and months unless space is limited.
- Skip ordinal endings: "July 3" not "July 3rd."
- Use lowercase am and pm with no space: 7pm / 7:30pm / 9am.
- Use time zones for scheduled events. Default: ET. Approved: ET, CT, MT, PT.
- Example: "Join us at 2pm ET."

### Acronyms
- Spell out on first use if the reader may not know the acronym.
- Well-known terms like SMS, ROI, and CTR do not need to be spelled out for Attentive's audience.
- Use e.g., and i.e., with the comma after each.

## Product, Brand, and Trademark Rules

Company name:
- Use: Attentive
- Do not use: Attentive Mobile, AttentiveMobile, or Attentive Mobile, Inc. outside legal contexts.

Possessives:
- Avoid "Attentive's AI," "Attentive's products."
- Prefer: "Attentive AI™," "Attentive products," "the Attentive platform."

Trademarks:
- two-tap™: always lowercase, never Two-Tap, Two-tap, twotap, or 2-tap.
- Attentive AI™: full trademarked name on first mention, then "Attentive AI." Never "AI" alone or "Attentive's AI."
- Two-Way Journeys™: full name on first mention, then "Two-Way Journeys." For general two-way communication, use "two-way."
- Attentive Concierge™: full name on first mention, then "Attentive Concierge." Never "Concierge" alone.
- LiveSMS™: full name on first mention, then "LiveSMS."

## AI Tool Instructions

### Core AI instruction
Write as Attentive: clear, human, perceptive, deliberate, agile, and warm. Lead with customer value and outcomes. Avoid jargon, hype, corporate filler, unsupported claims, fear-based framing, and overly casual language. Use concise sentences, natural contractions, active voice, approved Attentive terminology, and the grammar and mechanics rules in this guide.

### AI must always do this
- Sound like a helpful peer-mentor, not a professor or salesperson.
- Write to real people, not abstract audiences.
- Connect features to outcomes.
- Make the customer the hero.
- Explain complex ideas simply.
- Use Attentive-approved product names and terms.
- Keep claims grounded and specific.
- Respect the reader's time.
- Read naturally out loud.

### AI must never do this
- Invent statistics, product capabilities, customer names, or results.
- Use "leverage" or "utilize."
- Overuse "seamless," "unlock," "supercharge," "revolutionary," or "game-changing."
- Position AI as replacing marketers.
- Use Attentive Mobile instead of Attentive.
- Use unsupported superlatives.
- Write in a cold, corporate, or robotic tone.
- Use fear-based urgency.

### AI editing prompt
Revise the following copy to match Attentive's brand voice. Make it clearer, warmer, more concise, and more customer-centered. Use active voice, natural contractions, and approved terminology. Avoid jargon, hype, and unsupported claims. Preserve the original meaning unless clarity requires a stronger structure. After revising, briefly explain the most important changes.

### AI generation prompt
Create [asset type] for [audience] about [topic]. Write in Attentive's voice: perceptive, deliberate, agile, and human. Lead with customer value, connect the message to business outcomes, and keep the tone [tone needed]. Use concise language, active voice, natural contractions, and approved Attentive terminology. Avoid jargon, fear-based framing, unsupported claims, and corporate filler.

### AI QA prompt
Review the following copy for Attentive brand voice, style, grammar, mechanics, product naming, and terminology. Flag anything that sounds off-brand, unclear, too salesy, too generic, too technical, or inconsistent with the style guide. Then provide a revised version.

## Brand Voice Checklist

### Clarity and purpose
- Would someone outside our team immediately understand this?
- Does every sentence move the reader forward?
- Is there fluff to cut?
- Have simpler words replaced technical or industry-heavy language?
- Is the intended action or takeaway clear?

### Brand voice and personality
- Does this sound like a real person, not AI or a corporate robot?
- Does it show expertise without showing off?
- Is it warm and professional?
- Does it feel like Attentive understands the reader's challenge?

### Customer-centered thinking
- Does the copy focus on what the reader cares about?
- Does it connect technology to better customer experiences?
- Does it speak to outcomes, not just features?
- Does it remember there's a real person behind every data point?

### Strategic alignment
- Does this align with "Marketing made personal"?
- Does it balance innovation with practicality?
- Does it reinforce that technology amplifies human understanding?
- Does it build trust through both message and tone?

### Quality standards
- Has the copy been read aloud?
- Are there any words to cut?
- Are grammar, typos, and consistency checked?
- Would the writer feel proud to put their name on it?

### Final test
- If a busy marketer received this, would it respect their time?
- Does this feel helpful rather than salesy or interruptive?
- Is it easy for the reader to understand, say yes, or take action?

## Quick Before/After Examples

Generic to Attentive:
- Before: "Leverage our AI-powered platform to optimize omnichannel engagement across the customer lifecycle."
- After: "Use Attentive AI™ to reach customers with more relevant messages across SMS, email, RCS, and push."

Feature-first to outcome-first:
- Before: "Our platform includes advanced identity resolution and segmentation capabilities."
- After: "Recognize more shoppers, understand what they care about, and send messages that feel relevant in the moment."

Too cold to human:
- Before: "Attentive's AI-driven platform delivers enterprise-grade solutions."
- After: "Attentive helps marketers turn customer signals into messages that feel personal—and drive performance."

Too hypey to confident:
- Before: "Supercharge your marketing with revolutionary AI that transforms everything."
- After: "Let AI agents handle the busywork of personalization so your team can focus on strategy."

Too negative to supportive:
- Before: "Brands that fail to personalize will lose customer loyalty."
- After: "Brands that understand what each customer needs are better positioned to build lasting loyalty."
