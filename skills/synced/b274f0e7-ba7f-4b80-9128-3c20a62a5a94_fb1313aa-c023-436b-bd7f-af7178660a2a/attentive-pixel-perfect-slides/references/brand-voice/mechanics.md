# Grammar and mechanics

## Capitalization

Use **title case** for: case study titles, press release titles, blog post titles,
webinar titles, one-pager titles. Rule: capitalize nouns, pronouns, adjectives, verbs,
and all words four or more letters long. Capitalize both words in hyphenated terms.

Use **sentence case** for: website headlines, website subheads, blog subheads, ad
headlines, email subject lines, CTA buttons. Rule: capitalize only the first word and
proper nouns. **When in doubt, use sentence case.**

## Commas

Use the Oxford comma.

- Yes: "SMS, RCS, and email"
- No: "SMS, RCS and email"

For two items, do not use a comma before "and": "We help you understand customers and
build stronger relationships."

## Periods

Use periods for full sentences in body copy. Do **not** use periods in headlines,
subheadlines, or CTA buttons.

## Em dashes

Use true em dashes with **no spaces** around them.

- Yes: "Every signal matters—especially when timing is everything."
- No: "Every signal matters — especially when timing is everything."

Use em dashes for a brief aside, a thought extension, or added emphasis. Don't overuse
them — if a sentence gets too long, split it.

## Ampersands

Use ampersands sparingly. OK in official company/brand names, in social posts when
character count is tight, and in decks where space is limited. Avoid them in long-form
content, email body copy, and website body copy when "and" fits.

## Hyphens

Use a hyphen when two or more words act as one idea **before** a noun. No hyphen when
the phrase functions as a noun.

- Adjective (hyphen): "real-time data", "limited-time offer", "third-party integration"
- Noun (no hyphen): "data in real time", "available for a limited time", "working with a third party"

Common pairs — adjective / noun (or verb):

- real-time / real time
- on-demand / on demand
- sign-up / sign up
- opt-in / opt in
- opt-out / opt out
- set-up / set up
- third-party / third party
- best-in-class / best in class

## Numbers

- Spell out numbers less than 10 in body copy; use numerals for 10 and above.
- Spell out a number when it begins a sentence — unless it begins a title or headline.
- Use commas for numbers over three digits: 1,000; 24,500.
- Use the % symbol instead of spelling out "percent."
- Write out numbers over 1,000 unless space is limited. In tight spaces: 1K, 150K, 1M, 1B.

## Dates and time

- Spell out days and months unless space is limited: "July 3", "August 2", "September 1" — not "July 3rd".
- Use lowercase am and pm, no space: "7pm", "7:30pm", "9am", "9:45am" — not "7:00 PM".
- Use time zones for scheduled events; Attentive defaults to ET. Approved: ET, CT, MT, PT. Example: "Join us at 2pm ET."

## Acronyms and abbreviations

- Spell out on first use if the reader may not know it: "Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA)".
- Well-known terms like SMS and ROI don't need spelling out for Attentive's audience.
- Use "e.g.," for "for example" and "i.e.," for "in other words" — always include the comma after.

## Active voice

Use active voice whenever possible.

- Active: "Attentive helps marketers recognize more shoppers."
- Passive: "More shoppers are recognized by Attentive."
