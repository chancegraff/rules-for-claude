# Voice and tone

The **voice** stays consistent across every channel. **Tone** adapts based on
audience, context, and content type. Attentive's personality is: perceptive,
deliberate, agile, human.

## Contents

- Tone by situation
- Brand copy vs. platform copy

## The four voice traits

### Perceptive

We see what others miss. We read signals, spot patterns, and understand what drives
people — not just what they do, but why.

Sounds like: informed, context-aware, situational, attuned, nuanced, timely.

Shows up as: lead with insight before product; ground messages in what matters now;
choose specificity over generality; show understanding through restraint, not volume.

- Write this: "Your customers are already telling you what they need. Attentive helps you recognize the signal and respond while the moment still matters."
- Not this: "Our platform has advanced segmentation features."

### Deliberate

Every word has a job. We act with direction in product, messaging, and moments.

Sounds like: intentional, assured, decisive, measured, direct.

Shows up as: connect every claim to an outcome; cut anything that doesn't earn its
place; clear, confident language without overstatement; make decisions visible through
precise wording.

- Write this: "Every message should have a reason to exist—and a clear outcome to drive."
- Not this: "Unlock powerful solutions that maximize engagement."

### Agile

We move with people and adapt in real time across channels, audiences, and moments.

Sounds like: adaptive, continuous, steady, responsive, flexible with control.

Shows up as: write for motion, not static journeys; connect moments across channels;
show how experiences evolve without friction; emphasize continuity and relevance.

- Write this: "Every interaction informs the next, so each channel gets smarter instead of starting over."
- Not this: "Automate your marketing workflows across multiple channels."

### Human

Technology should amplify human understanding, not replace it. Behind every data
point is a person.

Sounds like: conversational, curious, approachable, warm, thoughtful, real.

Shows up as: write like you're advising a colleague; avoid jargon, hype, and scripts;
balance warmth with point of view; respect people's time, attention, and boundaries.

- Write this: "Your customers want to feel known—not followed around the internet."
- Not this: "Leverage data-driven orchestration to optimize consumer touchpoints."

## Tone by situation

Tone shifts by use case, but the voice stays the same.

- **Blog posts and thought leadership** — curious, strategic, forward-looking. For
  explaining market shifts, sharing perspective, helping marketers understand what's
  changing. Example: "Here's what the shift to omnichannel is actually doing to customer
  expectations—and what smart brands are doing about it."
- **Product marketing** — direct, specific, outcome-focused. For explaining product
  value, connecting capabilities to business results, web copy, one-pagers, launch copy,
  sales enablement. Example: "Know who's shopping. Reach them everywhere. Drive results
  that actually move the number."
- **Customer support and documentation** — patient, practical, clear. For instructions,
  explaining steps, reducing friction. Example: "Let's take this one step at a time.
  Here's exactly what to do first."
- **Sales materials** — confident, consultative, informed. For advising prospects,
  framing opportunities, explaining strategy. Example: "Here's what we're seeing work for
  brands like yours, and how we'd think about your specific situation."
- **Brand campaigns and awareness** — warm, human, emotionally resonant. For building
  belief, creating campaign lines, introducing the brand promise. Example: "Marketing
  that feels like a helpful conversation with someone who truly pays attention."

## Brand copy vs. platform copy

### Brand copy

Leads with the emotional promise. Warm, human, outcome-oriented in a customer-feeling
sense. Use for campaigns, brand moments, awareness content, narrative storytelling,
manifestos, social thought starters.

- "Marketing made personal."
- "Turn messages into moments people remember."

### Platform and product copy

Leads with results and proof. Direct, specific, outcome-oriented in a
business-performance sense. Use for web pages, sales decks, category pages, feature
pages, product announcements, competitive positioning.

- "Attentive identifies shoppers, personalizes every touchpoint, and acts across SMS, email, RCS, and push."
- "2.4x higher conversion when every channel works together." (Only use real, approved figures — never invent stats.)

## Channel guidance

### Website copy

Use sentence case for headlines and subheads, direct outcome-focused language, strong
hierarchy, clear CTAs. Prioritize what the reader gains, what the platform does, proof
or specificity, and plain language.

### Blog and thought leadership

Use title case for blog titles, sentence case for subheads, curious/strategic framing,
clear examples and takeaways. Prioritize education, timely insight, helpful guidance,
and reader value over product promotion.

### Email

Use sentence case subject lines, clear human openings, one primary message, short
paragraphs, direct CTA language. Avoid overly clever subject lines that obscure the
point, ampersands in body copy when "and" fits, and too many exclamation points.

### Social

Use clear hooks, natural language, minimal jargon, emojis sparingly and deliberately.
Social can be more conversational, but it should still sound professional and useful.

### Sales materials

Use confident, consultative language, specific outcomes, clear positioning, practical
framing. Avoid fear-based selling, unsupported competitive claims, and overly technical
explanations unless the audience needs them.

### Support and documentation

Use step-by-step clarity, patient practical tone, plain language, active voice. Avoid
overly friendly language that slows comprehension, product marketing claims, and
ambiguous instructions.
