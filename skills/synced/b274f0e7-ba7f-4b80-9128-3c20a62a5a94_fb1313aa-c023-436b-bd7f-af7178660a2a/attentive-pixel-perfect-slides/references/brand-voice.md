# Attentive Brand Voice

Write as Attentive: clear, human, perceptive, deliberate, agile, and warm. Lead
with customer value and outcomes. This skill is the source of truth for producing
on-brand Attentive copy across marketing, product, sales, support, and internal
comms.

## Pick a mode

Figure out which of three jobs the request is asking for, then follow that flow.
All three obey the same rules — only the deliverable differs.

- **Generate** — the user wants new copy from a topic, brief, or notes.
- **Edit** — the user has a draft and wants it rewritten to sound like Attentive.
- **QA** — the user wants existing copy checked against the brand system.

If the ask is ambiguous, default to Edit when a draft is present, otherwise Generate.

## Non-negotiables (apply in every mode)

These are the rules that break brand fastest, so keep them in working memory. The
full detail lives in the reference files.

- Use **Attentive** — never "Attentive Mobile" or "AttentiveMobile" outside legal contexts.
- Write like a real person. Use contractions. Cut corporate filler, jargon, and hype.
- Lead with customer value and outcomes, not features alone. Make the customer the hero.
- Make every word earn its place. If it doesn't clarify, guide, or persuade, cut it.
- Stay positive and supportive. No fear-based framing ("you'll fall behind").
- Never invent claims, statistics, customer names, or product details.
- Use active voice, the Oxford comma, and em dashes with no surrounding spaces.
- Get trademarks right on first mention: `Attentive AI™`, `two-tap™`, `Two-Way Journeys™`,
  `Attentive Concierge™`, `LiveSMS™`. Prefer `Attentive AI™` over "Attentive's AI".

Banned words that show up constantly: **leverage, utilize, synergy, ecosystem,
supercharge, seamless (as filler), unlock, revolutionary, game-changing.** Replace
with plain verbs — use, improve, boost, connect, build, drive, help, learn, understand.

## The four voice traits

The voice is constant; tone flexes by channel. Every piece should read as some
blend of these four. Full "write this / not this" examples in
`brand-voice/voice-and-tone.md`.

- **Perceptive** — lead with insight before product; ground in what matters now; specific over general.
- **Deliberate** — every word has a job; connect each claim to an outcome; confident, not overstated.
- **Agile** — write for motion; show how channels inform each other; emphasize continuity.
- **Human** — write like you're advising a colleague; warm, curious, real; respect the reader's time.

## Generate

1. Identify **asset type, audience, topic, and channel**. Channel sets the tone —
   look up the right tone in `brand-voice/voice-and-tone.md` (§ Tone by situation,
   § Channel guidance) before writing.
2. Decide **brand copy vs. platform copy**. Brand copy leads with the emotional
   promise (campaigns, awareness, manifestos). Platform copy leads with results and
   proof (web, decks, feature pages). See `brand-voice/voice-and-tone.md`.
3. Draft leading with customer value. Pull positioning, pillars, and proof language
   from `brand-voice/messaging.md` — do not invent stats or claims.
4. Apply `brand-voice/mechanics.md` (capitalization by asset type, punctuation,
   numbers, dates) and `brand-voice/terms-and-trademarks.md` (product names, first-mention
   trademarks, approved terms).
5. Self-check against `brand-voice/checklists.md` before delivering. Read it aloud —
   if it doesn't sound like something you'd say to help a colleague, revise.

## Edit

1. Preserve the author's intent and meaning; change structure only when clarity needs it.
2. Rewrite for the Attentive voice: clearer, warmer, more concise, more customer-centered.
   Active voice, natural contractions, approved terminology.
3. Strip banned words, jargon, hype, fear-based urgency, and unsupported claims. Swap
   feature-first framing for outcome-first (make the customer the hero).
4. Fix mechanics and terminology per `brand-voice/mechanics.md` and
   `brand-voice/terms-and-trademarks.md`.
5. Deliver the revised copy, then briefly explain the most important changes so the
   author learns the pattern.

## QA

Review copy for voice, style, grammar, mechanics, product naming, and terminology.
Flag anything off-brand, unclear, too salesy, too generic, too technical, or
inconsistent — then provide a corrected version.

Work through `brand-voice/checklists.md` (the brand voice checklist and the final QA
checklist). Report findings as a short list of issues (quote the phrase, name the
rule, give the fix), then the clean rewrite. Verify against
`brand-voice/terms-and-trademarks.md` and `brand-voice/mechanics.md` — these catch the
most common misses (banned terms, trademark first-mention, Oxford comma, spaced em
dashes, "Attentive Mobile", possessive "Attentive's AI").
