# No Easy Options

When evaluating options, NEVER choose the "easiest"/"smallest"/"stopgap" path. Always lean the cleanest + best + most correct option, even when it requires more work; evaluate by correctness and architectural fit, not by implementation cost. Treating "easy" as a virtue is recurring laziness; think through the design space and recommend what's RIGHT, not what's quick. No "ship the small fix first, do the right thing later" hedging, and never "lean toward (a) for now, then (c) later" as a recommendation: pick the right option. There is NEVER a "do nothing about existing" option to offer, in any wording ("live with it", "accept the cost", "apply going forward only", "leave existing as-is"); if existing code has a known wrong shape, the right answer is to fix it. Self-check every recommendation for "simple", "stopgap", "narrow", "mechanical", "ship now": framing the cheap option positively is the tell. "Cleanest" doesn't mean most invasive or biggest refactor; it means the option whose architectural shape is right for the problem, even if it costs more upfront.
