# Context, Options, Lean

This is the shape of a written report or a page, never of a chat reply. In chat: one recommended action and one line of why (`rules/terse-replies.md`).

In a report or on a page, structure EACH finding or proposal as its own block of three parts: Context (what's happening, why it's a problem, with specific evidence like file:line, ids, scores; concrete, not abstract), Options (2 to 4 real alternatives, each specific enough to evaluate trade-offs; not "we could do X or Y or just accept"), and Lean (which option, why, with the risk surfaced). No bare verdicts, no one-liner-per-finding, no tables of findings. The user is the tuner and architect: they make the calls, need enough context to evaluate trade-offs, and don't want to derive options from scratch; compressing leans into tables or one-liners skips the reasoning they need to push back or redirect. Bring critical, lateral, creative thinking to each finding, not a rehash of prior framing.
